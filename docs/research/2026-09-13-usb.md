# USB-C interface research — Devais (nRF52840 handheld)

Every fact below is tagged **VERIFIED** (with the source URL it came from) or **UNVERIFIED**.
No part number in this document was invented; each one was read off a manufacturer drawing,
a distributor page, or a KiCad footprint that cites the manufacturer drawing.

Date of research: 2026-09-13.

---

## 0. Two blocking findings — read these first

### F1. The XIAO nRF52840 does not break out D+/D-

**VERIFIED.** The Seeed XIAO nRF52840 castellated pads are: 5V, GND, 3V3, D0–D10, NFC1, NFC2
(plus RESET). There is no USB_DP / USB_DM / CC1 / CC2 pad. The onboard USB-C receptacle's
D+/D-/VBUS route straight to the nRF52840 die on the module.
Source: <https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/XIAO_BLE/> and the official schematic
<https://files.seeedstudio.com/wiki/XIAO-BLE/Seeed-Studio-XIAO-nRF52840-Sense-v1.1.pdf>

Consequence: a USB-C daughterboard carrying **data** (DFU/debug) to a XIAO is not possible
without bodge wires onto the module's own USB-C pads or the nRF's D+/D- pins. A daughterboard
carrying **power only** (VBUS → 5V pad) works fine.

If firmware DFU over the external port is a hard requirement, the choices are:
1. Expose the XIAO's own USB-C through the enclosure (a window in the shell) — no daughterboard.
2. Replace the XIAO with a bare nRF52840 module (MDBT50Q-1MV2, Ebyte E73-2G4M08S1C, etc.)
   on the main PCB, which does break out D+/D-. **UNVERIFIED** — I did not check which specific
   modules break out D+/D- as part of this task.
3. Keep the XIAO, and do DFU over BLE (Nordic buttonless DFU) instead of USB.

### F2. A 6.6 mm-wide daughterboard cannot hold a USB-C receptacle

**VERIFIED.** Every standard horizontal USB-C receptacle body is ~8.94 mm wide:
- GCT USB4105: body 8.94 mm wide (drawing dimension "8.94", recommended layout 8.64 mm across
  shell stakes). <https://datasheet.octopart.com/USB4105-GF-A-060-GCT-datasheet-192318489.pdf>
- HRO TYPE-C-31-M-12: F.Fab body outline −4.47…+4.47 mm = **8.94 mm wide** × 7.30 mm deep,
  with shell-stake pads centred at ±4.32 mm (1.0 mm wide → outer edge ±4.82 mm).
  <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-footprints/master/Connector_USB.pretty/USB_C_Receptacle_HRO_TYPE-C-31-M-12.kicad_mod>
- GCT USB4085 (through-hole): body outline −1.5…+7.45 = **8.95 mm wide** × 9.17 mm deep.
  <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-footprints/master/Connector_USB.pretty/USB_C_Receptacle_GCT_USB4085.kicad_mod>

Minimum practical daughterboard width is therefore **~10 mm** (8.94 body + stake pad overhang +
~0.3 mm edge clearance each side). Revise the daughterboard to about **10 × 13 mm**, or mount the
receptacle on the main PCB.

### 1.3 Recommendation

**Use GCT USB4105-GF-A-120, LCSC C5184243.**

Reasons:
- 20,000-cycle tested durability — 2× the USB-C spec minimum, and the highest of the JLCPCB-
  stocked candidates.
- Through-hole shell stakes with an explicit 1.20 mm stake length that suits a 1.0–1.6 mm board.
- 1,606 in stock and $0.53 @100 — the cheapest of the two 20K-cycle options.
- Standard SMT reflow at JLCPCB (JLCPCB marks GCT USB4105 "High" assembly difficulty with a
  small special-component fee — this is normal for every USB-C receptacle, including the XKB and
  HRO parts).
- Its plain-`-GF-A` sibling is out of stock at LCSC, so order the `-120` specifically.

**Fallback if C5184243 goes out of stock:** HRO TYPE-C-31-M-12 (C165948). 10,000 cycles instead
of 20,000, but 231k in stock, $0.10, identical 8.94 × 7.30 mm body, and the most widely proven
footprint in the hobby/JLCPCB world. Its PCB footprint is *not* pin-compatible with the GCT part,
so lay out a spin for each or pick one and commit.

**If you want maximum abuse tolerance and can pay for a THT step:** GCT USB4085-GF-A (C7095263),
all 16 contacts through-hole, 20,000 cycles.

## 2. Minimum USB 2.0 device circuit on USB-C

All facts in this section are **VERIFIED** from the USB Type-C Cable and Connector Specification
Release 2.0, August 2019:
<https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/USB%20Type-C%20Spec%20R2.0%20-%20August%202019.pdf>

### 2.1 CC1 and CC2: 5.1 kΩ each to GND — confirmed

> "Both CC1 and CC2 pins shall be independently terminated to ground through Rd."
> — §4.5.2.2.3.1 Unattached.SNK Requirements, p.171

Table 4-25, Sink CC Termination (Rd) Requirements, p.236:

| Rd implementation | Nominal value | Can detect power capability? | Max voltage on pin |
|---|---|---|---|
| ±20% voltage clamp | 1.1 V | No | 1.32 V |
| ±20% resistor to GND | 5.1 kΩ | **No** | 2.18 V |
| ±10% resistor to GND | 5.1 kΩ | **Yes** | 2.04 V |

**Design consequence:** use **5.1 kΩ ±1%** (0402, cheap, JLCPCB Basic). A ±20% part is legal for
attach detection but is explicitly *not* allowed to be used for reading the source's current
advertisement. See §6 below.

Two separate resistors are required — one per CC pin, never a single shared one. They must not
be bridged, because the source uses which CC sees Rd to determine plug orientation.

### 2.2 D+ / D- both pairs tied together — confirmed

USB Type-C R2.0, Table 2-1 / Table 2-2 notes (pp.31 and 34):

> "Contacts B6 and B7 should not be present in the USB Type-C plug. The receptacle side shall
> support the USB 2.0 differential pair present on Dp1/Dn1 or Dp2/Dn2. The plug orientation
> determines which pair is active. In one implementation, Dp1 and Dp2 may be shorted on the
> host/device as close to the receptacle as possible to minimize stub length; Dn1 and Dn2 may
> also be shorted. **The maximum shorting trace length should not exceed 3.5 mm.**"

Receptacle pin map (Table 2-1, p.31):

| Pin | Signal | Pin | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | GND | B12 | GND |
| A4 | VBUS | B9 | VBUS |
| A5 | **CC1** | B8 | SBU2 |
| A6 | **Dp1** | B7 | **Dn2** |
| A7 | **Dn1** | B6 | **Dp2** |
| A8 | SBU1 | B5 | **CC2** |
| A9 | VBUS | B4 | VBUS |
| A12 | GND | B1 | GND |

So: **A6 + B6 = D+**, **A7 + B7 = D−**. Note the crossover — B6 is Dp2 and B7 is Dn2, i.e. the
B-row order is mirrored. Getting this backwards swaps D+/D− in one plug orientation only, which
produces the classic "works one way up, dead the other way up" bug.

Keep the shorting trace ≤ 3.5 mm.

### 2.3 VBUS and GND

> "All VBUS pins shall be connected together within the USB Type-C plug and shall be connected
> together at the USB Type-C receptacle connector when the receptacle is in its mounted condition
> (e.g., all VBUS pins bussed together on the PCB)." Same requirement for all four GND pins.
> — Table 2-1 notes 4 and 5, p.31

Bus A4+A9+B4+B9 together; bus A1+A12+B1+B12 together.

### 2.4 Shield / shell

> "**The receptacle shell shall be connected to the PCB ground plane.**"
> — USB Type-C R2.0 §2.2 note 11, p.41

**Tie the shell directly to GND.** No RC network. The RC (typically 1 MΩ ‖ 4.7 nF) chassis-isolation
trick applies to a *chassis* ground that is separate from signal ground; a small handheld with a
single ground plane has no such separation, and the Type-C spec's "shall" is unconditional here.
All four shell stakes go to the same ground plane.

### 2.5 SBU1 / SBU2

> "Contacts A8 and B8 (SBU1 and SBU2) shall be not connected unless required for a specified
> purpose (e.g., Audio Adapter Accessory Mode)." — Table 2-2 note 3, p.34

Leave A8 and B8 as unconnected pads. Do not ground them, do not stub them.

### 2.6 VBUS bulk capacitance limit

USB Type-C R2.0 Table 4-3, Sink characteristics, p.143:

> "VBUS Capacitance — max **10 µF** — Capacitance between VBUS and GND pins on receptacle when
> not in Attached.SNK."

So the total capacitance hanging on VBUS at the receptacle must be **≤ 10 µF** before the sink
is attached (i.e. inrush limit). Practical value: **1 µF to 4.7 µF** of ceramic close to the
receptacle. See §3.3 for the charger-side view.

### 2.7 Minimum net list for the daughterboard

| Net | Receptacle pins | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| VBUS | A4, A9, B4, B9 (bussed) | JST SH pin, plus 1 µF local ceramic |
| GND | A1, A12, B1, B12 + shell stakes (bussed) | JST SH pin(s) |
| D+ | A6 + B6 (≤3.5 mm short) | JST SH pin |
| D− | A7 + B7 (≤3.5 mm short) | JST SH pin |
| CC1 | A5 | 5.1 kΩ ±1% to GND, **on the daughterboard** |
| CC2 | B5 | 5.1 kΩ ±1% to GND, **on the daughterboard** |
| SBU1/SBU2 | A8, B8 | not connected |

Put the Rd resistors on the daughterboard, not across the cable — the CC loop must be short and
must not be broken if the cable is unplugged during assembly.

That is a 4-conductor cable (VBUS, GND, D+, D−). If you also want the MCU to read the CC voltage
(§6), add a fifth wire from CC1 (or a resistor-divider-free direct tap) — but see §6 for why a
6-way JST SH with CC1 and CC2 separately is the better layout.

**Pin-to-pin hookup** (VERIFIED from the datasheet's Figure 1 functional diagram):

```
        USBLC6-2SC6  (SOT-23-6, top view)
        ┌───────────────┐
 D+ ────┤ 1  I/O1   6   ├──── D+   (pins 1 and 6 are the SAME net)
 GND ───┤ 2  GND    5   ├──── VBUS (pin 5 = VBUS rail clamp)
 D− ────┤ 3  I/O2   4   ├──── D−   (pins 3 and 4 are the SAME net)
        └───────────────┘
```

- Pin 1 ↔ pin 6 are one channel. Pin 3 ↔ pin 4 are the other.
- Route the signal **through** the part: connector-side D+ into pin 1, MCU-side D+ out of pin 6.
  Same for D− on pins 3 → 4. This puts the package physically in the signal path with no stub,
  which is what you want for ESD.
- Pin 2 → GND (short, wide, straight to the plane — the discharge return path).
- Pin 5 → VBUS. This is not optional: the internal steering diodes dump the strike into the VBUS
  rail, and the 6 V Zener between pin 5 and pin 2 is what actually clamps it. Leaving pin 5
  floating defeats the device.
- **Placement: on the daughterboard, directly at the receptacle, before the JST cable.** ESD
  protection sitting 50 mm of wire downstream protects nothing.

**Caveat (UNVERIFIED as a datasheet mandate):** "pin 1 = connector side, pin 6 = MCU side" is a
routing convention, not an electrical requirement — pins 1 and 6 are the same node. Follow the
convention anyway so the layout reads correctly.

### 3.3 VBUS TVS — do the datasheets require one?

**Answer: not a separate one, if you use USBLC6-2SC6 correctly.**

- **VERIFIED:** the USBLC6-2SC6 already contains a 6 V rail clamp between pin 5 (VBUS) and pin 2
  (GND); that is what the VCL = 12 V @ 1 A / 17 V @ 5 A figures describe. Wiring pin 5 to VBUS
  gives you VBUS protection as part of the same part.
- **VERIFIED:** TI SLVAF82B Table 7-1 recommends **ESD441 on VBUS** for a non-PD 5 V Type-C port.
  Adding one ESD441 on VBUS at $0.048 is cheap insurance and raises the VBUS contact rating to
  30 kV. Recommended but not mandatory.
- **VERIFIED:** a separate rail-matched TVS (TI's TVS0500 / TVS1400 / TVS1800 / TVS2200 / TVS3300)
  is only required if you support USB-PD above 5 V (SLVAF82B Table 8-1). This device is a 5 V
  sink, so **no PD-class TVS is needed**.
- **VERIFIED:** Nordic's own recommendation in place of a VBUS TVS is a **2.2 Ω series resistor**
  on VBUS "for improved immunity to transient overvoltage during VBUS connection… recommended for
  new designs" (nRF52840 PS §7.3, BOM designator R1). Fit it on the main board, in series with the
  nRF's VBUS/VDDH pin. Note it must carry the charge current if VBUS also feeds the charger —
  in that case run the charger from VBUS *before* the resistor, and let the resistor feed only
  the nRF's VBUS/VDDH pin.

### 3.4 VBUS input capacitance — the number

**VERIFIED** from the USB 2.0 Specification §7.2.4.1 "Inrush Current Limiting", quoted verbatim:

> "The maximum load (CRPB) that can be placed at the downstream end of a cable is **10 µF in
> parallel with 44 Ω**. The 10 µF capacitance represents any bypass capacitor directly connected
> across the VBUS lines in the function plus any capacitive effects visible through the regulator
> in the device… If more bypass capacitance is required in the device, then the device must
> incorporate some form of VBUS surge current limiting, such that it matches the characteristics
> of the above load."

**VERIFIED**, §7.2.4.2 "Dynamic Detach": "A minimum of **1.0 µF** is recommended for bypass across
VBUS" at the device end, to stop flyback-induced polarity reversal on hot unplug.

USB Type-C R2.0 Table 4-3 restates the ceiling for sinks: VBUS capacitance max **10 µF** when not
in Attached.SNK (§2.6 above).

**Design values:**
- Daughterboard, right at the receptacle: **100 nF + 1 µF** ceramic. Small, stays well under the
  limit, gives the ESD clamp a local return.
- Main board: the rest of the bulk, up to a **total across both boards of ≤ 10 µF**. Nordic's
  reference BOM asks for 4.7 µF on VBUS plus 4.7 µF after the series resistor (9.4 µF) — that
  plus 1.1 µF on the daughterboard is 10.5 µF and technically over. Either drop the daughterboard
  cap to 100 nF only, or use 2.2 µF for Nordic's C6/C20 instead of 4.7 µF, or add inrush limiting.
- Anything above 10 µF (e.g. a big cap on the charger input) requires a soft-start / inrush
  limiter to be spec-compliant.

### 3.5 Does ESD-diode leakage on CC break the Rd divider?

**UNVERIFIED as a published warning.** TI's SLVAF82B makes no mention of Rd, CC leakage, or a
leakage budget. Nexperia's AN10753 predates Type-C entirely. No manufacturer app note stating
this was found.

The arithmetic from verified datasheet leakage figures says it is a non-issue for these parts:

| Part | Leakage | Error into 5.1 kΩ |

Against thresholds separated by 440–570 mV (0.66 V / 1.23 V, §6.1), even the USBLC6's 1 µA

small (100 nF – 1 µF) and let the main board carry the bulk.

### 4.2 External series resistors on D+/D−? Pull-up on D+?

| Symbol | Description | Min | Typ | Max | Units |

| RUSB,PU,ACTIVE | Pull-up on D+, bus active | 1425 | 2300 | 3090 | Ω |

Series resistors: the PS does not mention external series resistors on D+/D−, consistent with a

### 4.3 Trace impedance guidance

**UNVERIFIED — Nordic publishes none in the nRF52840 PS.** A full-text search of the PS for

The only Nordic-sourced statement found is a DevZone staff reply (**UNVERIFIED as primary spec**):
> "For long tracks, stick to the USB recommended 90 ohm differential impedance. Keeping them as a
> matched pair is ok." — same DevZone thread as above.

Use generic USB 2.0 practice: 90 Ω ±15% differential (USB 2.0 spec §7.1.1.1), routed as a tight
pair with continuous reference ground, length-matched. At 12 Mbit/s full speed this is a
comfort measure, not a hard requirement — see §5.

### 4.4 32 MHz crystal accuracy for USB

Two different numbers; do not conflate them. Both **VERIFIED**.

1. **USB-specific:** §6.35.14.1 USB Electrical Specification —
   `fUSB,CLK` = 48 MHz typ; **`fUSB,TOL` = "Accuracy of local clock, USB active" — max ±1000 ppm**;
   `TUSB,JITTER` = ±1 ns max. Footnote: the local clock may be stopped during USB suspend.
2. **HFXO general table (§5.4.4.2), radio-driven, not USB-driven:**
   - fTOL_HFXO (2.4 GHz proprietary): ±60 ppm
   - fTOL_HFXO_BLE (packet ≤200 bytes): ±40 ppm
   - fTOL_HFXO_BLE_LP (packet >200 bytes): ±30 ppm
   - CL_HFXO max 12 pF; C0_HFXO max 7 pF; RS_HFXO max 60 Ω (3 pF < C0 ≤ 7 pF) / 100 Ω (C0 ≤ 3 pF);
     PD_HFXO max 100 µW
   - There is **no `fTOL_HFXO_USB` row** — ±1000 ppm is Nordic's only USB-specific tolerance.

**Practical conclusion: BLE governs the crystal, not USB.** ±1000 ppm (0.1%) for USB is 25×
looser than the ±40 ppm BLE needs. Nordic's own reference BOM uses "XTAL SMD 2016, 32 MHz,
Cl = 8 pF, Total Tol: ±40 ppm". Spec a ±30 to ±40 ppm total-tolerance 32 MHz crystal and USB is
automatically satisfied. (If you use a XIAO or a pre-certified module, the crystal is already
chosen and correct.)

### 4.5 HFXO must be running for USB

**VERIFIED.**
> "The USBD will not be able to respond to USB traffic unless HFXO is enabled and stable."
> — §6.35.7.1

> "...the crystal oscillator (HFXO) in CLOCK may be disabled by software during the USB suspend,
> while the USB pull-up is disconnected, or when VBUS is not present. Software must explicitly
> enable it at any other time."

The internal 64 MHz RC (HFINT) tolerance is ±1.5% typ / ±8% max — 15× to 80× worse than the
±1000 ppm USB requirement, so HFINT cannot be used for USB. (This is an inference from two

documented for HFXO in the PS — **UNVERIFIED** that it is unsupported; simply not found.

## 5. Full-speed USB over ~50 mm of unshielded AWG28 through a JST SH

**Verdict: fine. This is well inside every relevant budget, by one to two orders of magnitude.**

### 5.1 What the USB 2.0 spec actually constrains

USB 2.0 Specification, §7.1.1.1 Full-speed (12 Mb/s) Driver Characteristics:
<https://usb.org/sites/default/files/usb_20_20190524.zip> (file `usb_20.pdf`)

- **VERIFIED:** "A full-speed USB connection is made through a shielded, twisted pair cable with a
  differential characteristic impedance (Z0) of 90 Ω ±15%, a common mode impedance (ZCM) of 30 Ω
  ±30%, and a maximum one-way delay (TFSCBL) of **26 ns**."
- **VERIFIED:** §7.1.2.1 — "The rise and fall times [for full-speed] must be between **4 ns and
  20 ns** and matched to within ±10%."
- **VERIFIED:** §7.1.1.1 — driver impedance ZDRV between 28 Ω and 44 Ω.

Those cable requirements apply to a **detachable USB cable** — the thing that plugs into the
receptacle. Your 50 mm run is *inside* the device, behind the receptacle. It is part of the
device's own interconnect, exactly like a PCB trace, and the spec's cable clauses do not
literally apply to it. What does apply is the total signal budget.

Numbers for 50 mm of wire:
- Propagation delay ≈ 50 mm / (0.66 × 3×10⁸ m/s) ≈ **0.25 ns**, against a 26 ns one-way budget.
- One full-speed bit is 83.3 ns. Edge rate is 4–20 ns. A 0.25 ns discontinuity is invisible.
- A reflection from an impedance mismatch settles in 2 × 0.25 ns = 0.5 ns, i.e. long before the
  4 ns minimum edge even finishes rising.

### 5.2 Published evidence that unshielded internal cable works

**Microchip AN1579, "USB 3.0 Internal Cable and Connector Evaluation" (2013)** —
<https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/en/AppNotes/00001579A.pdf>

- **VERIFIED:** Microchip tested plain ribbon cable at lengths from 40 mm to 440 mm against
  **USB 3.0 (5 Gbit/s) transmitter compliance**, and reports: "All cable lengths **up to 300 mm
  passed** the USB 3.0 TX tests." Measured eye height 158.9 mV against a 100 mV limit, total
  jitter 33.2 ps against a 132 ps limit — passing with large margin.
- **VERIFIED:** unshielded FFC with a ZIF connector also passed USB 3.0 TX compliance (180 mV eye
  vs 195 mV for a proper shielded Amphenol assembly; jitter actually *better* on the FFC).
- **VERIFIED:** "The most popular ribbon cables (i.e., 26AWG wire, 0.050" spacing and common PVC
  insulation) provide 120 ohms impedance for any two adjacent wires. However, with copper tape on
  one side, 90 ohms impedance is achievable."

If 300 mm of unshielded ribbon passes 5 Gbit/s, 50 mm of twisted AWG28 at 12 Mbit/s — **416×
slower** — is not a signal-integrity question.

### 5.3 JST SH connector — VERIFIED specs

From the JST SH datasheet, <https://www.jst-mfg.com/product/pdf/eng/eSH.pdf>:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Pitch | 1.0 mm |
| **Current rating** | **1.0 A AC/DC (AWG #28)** |
| Voltage rating | 50 V AC/DC |
| Applicable wire | **AWG #32 to AWG #28** |
| Insulation O.D. | φ0.4 mm to φ0.8 mm |
| Contact resistance | 20 mΩ initial, 40 mΩ after environmental tests |
| Insulation resistance | 100 MΩ min |
| Temperature | −25 °C to +85 °C |

**The real constraint is current, not signal integrity.** 1.0 A per contact. If the 18650 charger
draws more than ~0.8 A from VBUS, double up: use a 6-way SH with two VBUS contacts and two GND
contacts in parallel, or throttle the charger to 500 mA. Check this against your charge IC's
programmed ICHG.

Also check against Nordic's RSOURCE,VBUS = 6 Ω (§4.1). Two SH contacts + 50 mm AWG28 round trip
is roughly 2 × (0.020 + 0.011) ≈ 0.06 Ω. No problem.

### 5.4 Practical rules for the cable and the layout

1. **Keep D+ and D− on physically adjacent contacts** of the JST SH, with a **GND contact
   immediately beside them** — ideally GND on both sides. Suggested 6-way SH pinout:
   `1 = VBUS, 2 = VBUS, 3 = GND, 4 = D+, 5 = D−, 6 = GND`.
   That gives two paralleled VBUS contacts (2 A), a GND return adjacent to the pair on both
   sides, and no signal next to a switching power line.
2. **Twist D+ and D− together** over the run — even a loose hand twist of a few turns per
   centimetre. This is the single cheapest improvement and costs nothing in assembly.
3. **Do not route D+/D− next to the speaker leads.** The MAX98357A output is a Class-D PWM square
   wave at hundreds of kHz with hundreds of mA of edge current. Keep the USB cable and the
   speaker cable physically separated inside the enclosure, crossing at right angles if they must
   cross.
4. **Keep it short.** 50 mm is already good. Do not let it grow to 150 mm "for service loop".
5. **Length-match D+ and D−** — cut them to the same length. With hand-crimped SH leads, cut both
   wires from the same jig.
6. **Put the ESD array on the daughterboard**, right at the receptacle, before the cable. ESD
   protection placed behind 50 mm of wire protects nothing; the inductance of the wire is what the
   strike sees.
7. **Put the Rd resistors on the daughterboard too** (§2.7).
8. **Put the VBUS bulk capacitance on the main board**, with only 100 nF – 1 µF on the
   daughterboard, to stay under the Type-C 10 µF sink limit (§2.6).

### 5.5 Evidence against? 

None found. The commonly cited "USB requires a shielded cable" (USB 2.0 §6.4.1) is a requirement
on **cable assemblies sold as USB cables** — the thing between two connectors. It has never been
applied to intra-device wiring, which is why every phone, laptop, and games controller routes USB
over an unshielded internal FPC. Microchip's AN1579 is the closest thing to a published test of
exactly this, and it passes at 5 Gbit/s.

The one genuine risk is **EMC certification**, not function: an unshielded 50 mm pair carrying
12 Mbit/s inside a plastic enclosure is a small radiator. At full speed with 4–20 ns edges the
harmonics roll off fast, and 50 mm is a poor antenna below 1 GHz. **UNVERIFIED** — I found no
data on radiated emissions from short internal USB FS wiring; if you go to CE/FCC testing, budget
for the possibility of adding a common-mode choke on D+/D− at the main-board end.

### 6.1 The CC voltage table

All **VERIFIED** from USB Type-C R2.0:
<https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/USB%20Type-C%20Spec%20R2.0%20-%20August%202019.pdf>

**Table 4-24, Source CC Termination (Rp) Requirements, p.235** — what the source presents:

|---|---|---|---|
| Default USB Power | 80 µA ±20% | 56 kΩ ±20% | 36 kΩ ±20% |
| 1.5 A @ 5 V | 180 µA ±8% | 22 kΩ ±5% | 12 kΩ ±5% |
| 3.0 A @ 5 V | 330 µA ±8% | 10 kΩ ±5% | 4.7 kΩ ±5% |

**Table 4-36, Voltage on Sink CC pins (Multiple Source Current Advertisements), p.241** —

| Detection | Min voltage | Max voltage | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| vRa | −0.25 V | 0.15 V | 0.2 V |
| vRd-Connect | 0.25 V | 2.04 V | — |
| **vRd-USB (Default USB)** | **0.25 V** | **0.61 V** | **0.66 V** |
| **vRd-1.5 (1.5 A)** | **0.70 V** | **1.16 V** | **1.23 V** |
| **vRd-3.0 (3.0 A)** | **1.31 V** | **2.04 V** | — |

For comparison, **Table 4-35 (Default USB Type-C Current only), p.241** — for a sink that does
*not* implement current detection: vRa −0.25…0.15 V (threshold 0.2 V), vRd-Connect 0.25…2.18 V.

**Firmware decision rule** (directly from the Threshold column):

```
V_CC < 0.20 V  -> nothing attached (or Ra / powered cable)
0.20 V <= V_CC < 0.66 V  -> attached, Default USB Power  (500 mA USB 2.0 / 900 mA USB 3)
0.66 V <= V_CC < 1.23 V  -> attached, 1.5 A @ 5 V
V_CC >= 1.23 V           -> attached, 3.0 A @ 5 V
```

Sample the CC pin that is *not* near 0 V — only one of CC1/CC2 sees Rp, the other floats near
ground (or sees Ra if a powered cable is attached). So read **both** CC1 and CC2, take the higher,
and that also gives you plug orientation for free.

### 6.2 Rd tolerance — the trap

**VERIFIED**, Table 4-25, p.236 (reproduced in §2.1): a 5.1 kΩ **±20%** Rd is marked
"Can detect power capability? **No**". Only a **±10% (or tighter) 5.1 kΩ** resistor is permitted
to be used for reading the advertisement, and its max pin voltage drops from 2.18 V to 2.04 V.

**Use 5.1 kΩ ±1%.** It costs the same and removes the question. A ±5% part is also fine.

### 6.3 Can an MCU ADC read the CC pin without disturbing Rd?

**Yes, with two conditions. VERIFIED for the nRF52840 SAADC.**

The CC node is a divider: source Rp (10 k / 22 k / 56 k, or a current source) from ~5 V, against
your Rd = 5.1 kΩ to ground. Its **Thévenin source impedance is at most 5.1 kΩ** (it is
Rp ‖ Rd, always less than Rd).

nRF52840 PS §6.23.1.2, "Acquisition time", Table 41:

| TACQ [µs] | Maximum source resistance [kΩ] |
|---|---|
| 3 | 10 |
| 5 | 40 |
| 10 | 100 |
| 15 | 200 |
| 20 | 400 |
| 40 | 800 |

A 5.1 kΩ source is inside the **3 µs** row (10 kΩ max) — the SAADC's shortest acquisition time
already handles it with 2× margin. No buffering, no op-amp, no extra divider.
Source: <https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/6469/NRF52840-DK.pdf>
(live: <https://docs.nordicsemi.com/bundle/ps_nrf52840/page/saadc.html>)

Range: max CC voltage with a ±10% Rd is 2.04 V. The nRF52840 SAADC with the internal 0.6 V
reference and gain 1/6 gives a 3.6 V full scale — 2.04 V fits comfortably, no external divider
(an external divider would *both* load Rd and be unnecessary).

The two conditions:
1. **The ADC's own DC leakage must be negligible against the 5.1 kΩ.** A 1% error on Rd needs
   leakage below about 0.6 V / (100 × 5.1 kΩ) ≈ 1.2 µA at the worst-case working point.
   **UNVERIFIED** — I did not find an explicit SAADC input leakage figure in the nRF52840 PS.
   The SAADC is a switched-capacitor sampler that presents a high-Z input between samples, and
   Nordic's own guidance permits source resistances up to 800 kΩ (157× your 5.1 kΩ), which is
   only meaningful if leakage is far below 1 µA. Treat as very likely fine; verify on hardware by
   measuring the CC voltage with and without the ADC pin connected.
2. **Any ESD diode you put on the CC line adds its own leakage to the same node.** This is the
   real-world failure mode — see §3. Pick a CC-line ESD part with sub-100 nA leakage, or leave CC
   unprotected (it is a low-speed, resistively-terminated node, and the 5.1 kΩ itself is some
   protection).

Also note: **the CC pins are on the daughterboard.** To read them from the main-board MCU you must
carry CC1 and CC2 across the JST SH as well, which pushes you to a 6-way or 8-way SH. The Rd
resistors stay on the daughterboard; only the sense taps cross the cable, and the added wire
resistance (~0.01 Ω) is irrelevant against 5.1 kΩ.

  state it in what I could fetch.

**Verdict: skip it.** Two 5.1 kΩ ±1% resistors (~$0.002) plus two ADC readings do the same job
for this device. The TUSB320LAI earns its $0.39 when you need autonomous DRP/role detection,
orientation switching for a mux, or I²C interrupt-driven attach handling with the MCU asleep.
A sink-only device with an MCU that is awake whenever it is plugged in does not.

The one thing you lose: the TUSB320 debounces attach/detach in hardware and drives an INT pin,
so the MCU does not have to poll the CC ADC. With the resistor approach, gate your CC sampling
on the nRF's own USBDETECTED event (VBUS rising past 3.4–4.3 V, §4.1) — then you sample the CC
pins only when something is actually plugged in, and burn no idle current.
