### 1.3 Verdict on "3 mm at each end"

**3.0 mm per end (71.0 mm inside) is marginally too little — but only just.** Two workable
answers:

**Option A — coil springs, ~3.4 mm per end (recommended if you want to keep the current layout)**

- Negative: **Keystone 5201** (coil spring with solder tab) — DigiKey **36-5201-ND**, $0.27 @1,
  $0.225 @10, 48,347 in stock. **VERIFIED** ([DigiKey 5201](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/keystone-electronics/5201/316365))
- Positive: **Keystone 5223** (button with solder tab) — DigiKey **36-5223-ND**, $0.14 @1.
  **VERIFIED** ([DigiKey 5223](https://www.digikey.com/product-detail/en/keystone-electronics/5223/36-5223-ND/316374))
- Keystone's L for this pair is 71.6 mm for a 65.0 mm cell. Real cells are up to 65.25–65.30 mm
  (see §3), so **make the inside compartment 71.9–72.0 mm**, i.e. **~3.4 mm per end**.
  That is 0.4–0.5 mm more than your current 3 + 3 mm. **Change the cradle from 71.0 to 72.0 mm
  inside.**
- Both parts have a solder tab that exits the cradle, which is what you want for wire leads.
  Plate is 11.2 × 12.0 mm, so the end wall needs a flat ≥12 × 12 mm landing plus a tab slot.

If you do not need solder tabs, the tabless equivalents are **5202** (−, DigiKey **36-5202-ND**,
$0.33 @1 / $0.272 @10 / $0.2254 @100, 41,329 in stock) and **5224** (+, DigiKey **36-5224-ND**,
$0.18 @1 / $0.149 @10 / $0.1236 @100, 29,650 in stock), L = 71.4 mm. **VERIFIED**

**Option B — leaf contacts, ~2 mm per end (shortest, and easiest to print)**

- Negative: **Keystone 209** — DigiKey **36-209-ND**, $0.30 @1, $0.25 @10, $0.21 @100,
  93,033 in stock. **VERIFIED** ([DigiKey 209](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/keystone-electronics/209/151583))
- Positive: **Keystone 228** — DigiKey **36-228-ND**, $0.25 @1, $0.205 @10, $0.1704 @100,
  109,727 in stock. **VERIFIED** ([DigiKey 228](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/keystone-electronics/228/528660))
- Keystone L = **68.8 mm** inside. With a 65.3 mm cell, set the inside to **69.1 mm**,
  i.e. **~1.9 mm per end**. This is *shorter* than your 3 mm budget and saves ~2.9 mm of
  enclosure length versus Option A.
- They snap over the end-wall **edge**, so print the two end walls **1.6 mm thick** with the
  edge open (the catalog says .060–.070 in = 1.52–1.78 mm, with barbs that bite into the wall).
  No slot geometry needed — this is the friendliest option for FDM.

**Critical caveat (applies to both):** the compartment length must be matched to the *chosen*
contact pair, not set to a generic "3 mm each end". If you fit 209/228 into a 71.0 mm
compartment, the leaf spring is under-deflected by ~2.2 mm and may make no contact at all.

**Mouser part numbers:** Mouser's Keystone prefix is `534-`, so these are `534-5201`, `534-5202`,
`534-5223`, `534-5224`, `534-209`, `534-228`. Only **534-1043** was seen in a search result;
the rest are **UNVERIFIED** (Mouser product pages time out from this environment). DigiKey
numbers above are all verified.

### 1.4 Compressed-length data that Keystone does *not* publish

Keystone publishes spring **free** length (8.5 / 10.5 / 12.7 mm) and the installed **L**
dimension, but **not** a solid/compressed height for the coil. Any "compressed length" figure
would be **UNVERIFIED**. Use the L dimension — it already encodes the intended deflection.

### 2.3 Comparison against a 40 × 40 × 150 mm enclosure with a 26 × 126 mm PCB

- **Length:** 77.1 mm (1043) or 76.96 mm (MPD) vs. your custom cradle at **72.0 mm** inside
  (Option A) or **69.1 mm** (Option B). The custom cradle saves **5–8 mm** of enclosure length.
  Nothing here is close to the 150 mm limit, so length is not the deciding factor.
- **Width — this is the constraint.** With ~2 mm walls a 40 mm outer enclosure gives ~36 mm
  inside. Holder 20.7 mm + PCB 26 mm side by side = **46.7 mm > 36 mm**. The cell and the PCB
  **cannot sit side by side across the width.** They must be stacked: mount the holder *on* the
  26 mm-wide PCB (20.7 mm holder width fits on a 26 mm board), giving a stack of
  1.6 mm board + 18.0–21.4 mm holder ≈ **19.6–23 mm** in a 36 mm interior. That works with room
  for the 26 mm board width in the other axis.
- Same conclusion for a custom cradle: 18.6 mm cell + 2 × ~1.5 mm wall ≈ 21.6 mm wide.
  (Arithmetic above is mine; the input dimensions are VERIFIED, the layout conclusion is
  **UNVERIFIED** engineering judgement.)

### 3.2 Panasonic / Sanyo NCR18650GA — alternative

Source: [Panasonic datasheet](https://www.orbtronic.com/content/Datasheet-specs-Sanyo-Panasonic-NCR18650GA-3500mah.pdf). **VERIFIED**

- Rated 3,300 mAh @20 °C; minimum 3,350 mAh, typical 3,450 mAh @25 °C
- Nominal 3.6 V; charge CC-CV 4.20 V, **standard current 1,475 mA**, 270 min
- **Height max 65.30 mm**, **diameter max Ø18.50 mm** (with tube), weight max 48.0 g
- Charge temp 10…+45 °C, discharge −20…+60 °C
- **Max continuous discharge is NOT stated in this datasheet.** The discharge-rate curves are
  plotted to 10 A, and every distributor calls it "10 A". The 10 A figure is therefore
  **UNVERIFIED against the manufacturer datasheet**.
- At 65.30 mm max it is exactly at your limit — zero margin. NKON also flags this cell as
  withdrawn from production. **Prefer the 35E.**

### 3.3 LG INR18650 MJ1 — second alternative, with a caveat

Source: [LG Chem product spec LRB-PS-CY3500_MJ1 Rev 1, 2016-06-30](https://power.tenergy.com/content/datasheet/30708_datasheet.pdf). **VERIFIED**

- Nominal 3,500 mAh, minimum 3,350 mAh; nominal voltage 3.635 V
- Standard charge 0.5C (1,675 mA) CC to 4.2 V, 50 mA cut-off; max charge current 1.0C (3,350 mA)
- **Max discharge current 10 A**; discharge cut-off 2.5 V; weight max 49.0 g
- **Height 65.1 ±0.2 mm (max 65.3 mm)** — fits
- **Diameter 18.5 +0.15/−0.3 mm → max Ø18.65 mm** — this **exceeds your 18.6 mm envelope.**

### 3.4 Design consequence for the cradle bore

Your stated envelope is Ø18.6 mm. Cell maxima are 18.50 (GA), 18.55 (35E), **18.65 (MJ1)**.
Add FDM shrink/elephant-foot and a heat-shrink wrap and 18.6 mm is too tight.
**Open the cradle bore to ≥19.0 mm** (19.0–19.2 mm recommended). **Arithmetic is mine;
the cell maxima are VERIFIED.**

### 3.5 Why a protected cell will not fit

A protected cell is an unprotected cell plus a small PCB (DW01-class IC + dual FET) welded to
the negative end, a nickel strip up the side, and a button cap over the positive end, then
re-wrapped. That adds length at both ends. Keystone's own layout page carries the warning
*"Built in circuit protection may increase length of battery, check battery specifications for
exact battery length"* (**VERIFIED, K75p42**), and MPD sells BK-18650-PC2 explicitly as a holder
"for protected 18650 batteries (PCB)" at 76.96 mm long (**VERIFIED, MPD datasheet**). Typical
protected-cell length is 67–70 mm; **that specific 67–70 mm range is UNVERIFIED** — I found it
only in retailer copy, not in a manufacturer spec. What *is* verified is that the cell envelope
you have (65.0 mm nominal, 65.3 mm max) is sized for a bare cell, and that adding a protection
PCB and button cap increases length, so a protected cell cannot go into a 65 mm pocket.
Since the design already has an on-board DW01A/BQ297xx protection stage, a protected cell would
also double up the protection.

**Reverse-insertion prevention — what is commercially used:**

Keystone sells **polarized** variants of its 18650 holders explicitly for this:
**1042P / 1043P** (leaf spring polarized) and **1130 / 1131** (coil spring polarized), described
in the catalog as *"Polarized holders available for protection against improper battery
installation"* and *"Accommodates cells with or without built-in PCB protection circuits"*.
**VERIFIED (K75p29).**

The *mechanism* — a small positive button recessed inside a raised insulating collar, so only a
raised positive cap can reach it while a reversed flat negative face bottoms out on the collar —
is the standard implementation and matches the idea in your brief, but Keystone's published
material states only that the holders are polarized, not the geometry. **The mechanism
description is UNVERIFIED.**

For a printed cradle, the cheap equivalent is to print a ~1 mm-tall insulating ring around the
positive contact so the contact face sits below the ring, and to make the negative end wall flat.
This is a design suggestion, **UNVERIFIED** against any specific product.

## 5. Fusing: series PTC in addition to the protection IC?

**Yes — a series PPTC at the cell is standard commercial practice even when a DW01A/BQ297xx +
back-to-back FET stage is present**, because the PPTC covers a different failure mode: it is an
*overtemperature* device thermally coupled to the cell, and it still acts if the protection FETs
fail short.

Bourns' battery-pack white paper states it directly: *"Integrating Bourns Multifuse PPTC devices
is an optimal overtemperature protection solution for battery packs, battery cells and
specifically for single-cell Li-ion battery packs. In order to provide reliable protection, the
PPTC device is mounted in such a way that it is linked thermally with the cell"*, and
*"The … PPTC resettable fuse provides overcurrent protection beyond the battery management IC."*
Their Figure 1 shows the PPTC in series with the cell alongside the protection IC and FETs.
**VERIFIED** ([Bourns white paper, Farnell-hosted PDF](https://uk.farnell.com/wcsstore/ExtendedSitesCatalogAssetStore/cms/asset/pdf/common/bourns/addressing-li-ion-battery-pack-and-battery-cell-reliability-threats.pdf))

Whether IEC 62133 *mandates* a PPTC in addition to a protection circuit: **not established.**
The standard is cited by Bourns as one of the applicable regulatory tests, but I found no
primary text requiring a PPTC as such. **UNVERIFIED.**

**Example part (verified, with LCSC number):**

- **Littelfuse 1812L110/33MR** — PPTC, 1812, **1.1 A hold / 1.95 A trip**, 33 V,
  **LCSC C142747**, ~$0.125 ea (5+). **VERIFIED** ([LCSC C142747](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/Resettable-Fuses_Littelfuse-1812L110-33MR_C142747.html))
- Also seen at LCSC/JLCPCB: PTTC **SMD1812P110TF** (1.1 A hold, 2.2 A trip), JLCPCB part
  **C3102**. **VERIFIED-as-listed** ([JLCPCB](https://jlcpcb.com/partdetail/PTTC_Polytronics_Tech-SMD1812P110TF/C3102)); its LCSC C-number was not confirmed.
- Bourns **MF-MSMF110-2** (1812, 1.1 A hold / 2.2 A trip, 6 V) exists at Newark/TME; **no LCSC
  number found — UNVERIFIED at LCSC.**

Sizing note: 1.1 A hold suits a device whose peak is the 200–500 mA speaker draw. If your
inrush or amplifier peak exceeds ~1 A sustained, step up to a 1.5–2 A hold part. Mount the PPTC
touching or immediately adjacent to the cell, in series with the cell positive lead, ahead of the
protection FETs.
