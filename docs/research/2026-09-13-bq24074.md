# BQ24074 — 1-cell Li-ion charger with power path (QFN-16 RGT)

Application: 5 V USB-C (no PD), one unprotected 18650 (~3400 mAh), 300–500 mA charge,
MCU-selectable input limit, ~600 mA peak system load, 10 k NTC on cell, MCU reads status.

## Correction to the task premise

**The BQ24074 has no TD pin and no SYSOFF pin.** Pin 15 on the '74 is **ITERM**
(programmable termination current). DS Table 7-1 and the Device Comparison Table:

| Variant | Pin 15 "optional function" |
|---|---|
| BQ24072, BQ24073 | TD (termination disable) |
| **BQ24074** | **ITERM** |
| BQ24075, BQ24079 | SYSOFF (battery disconnect) |

If you need a hardware battery-disconnect (SYSOFF) you must use the BQ24075, not the '74.
See the fallback section.

### ISET — fast-charge current (pin 16)

DS Eq 2: `ICHG = KISET / RISET`. `KISET = 890 AΩ` typ (min 797, max 975). Valid R_ISET range 590 Ω – 8.9 kΩ.

| Target I_CHG | R_ISET exact | E96 1 % value | Actual I_CHG (typ K) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 mA | 2966.7 Ω | **2.94 kΩ** | 303 mA (CALCULATED) |
| 400 mA | 2225 Ω | **2.21 kΩ** | 403 mA (CALCULATED) |
| 500 mA | 1780 Ω | **1.78 kΩ** | 500 mA (CALCULATED) |

The ±10 % spread on K_ISET (797–975 AΩ) dominates: at R=1.78 k the guaranteed range is 448–548 mA.

ISET doubles as a charge-current monitor. DS Eq 3: `VISET = ICHARGE / 400 × RISET`.
At 500 mA with 1.78 kΩ that is 2.225 V — readable by an MCU ADC (CALCULATED). Do not load
the node; buffer or use a high-impedance ADC input, and note the reading is only valid while
charging.

Pre-charge current: `IPRECHG = KPRECHG / RISET`, `KPRECHG = 88 AΩ` typ — i.e. ~10 % of fast charge.

### ILIM — programmable input current limit (pin 12)

DS Eq 1: `IIN-MAX = KILIM / RILIM`. Valid R_ILIM range **1.1 kΩ – 8 kΩ**, limit range 200 mA – 1.5 A.

**K_ILIM discrepancy in the DS — flagging it.** The Electrical Characteristics table gives
`KILIM` typ = **1610 AΩ** (min 1500, max 1720) for the 500 mA–1.5 A band, and typ **1525 AΩ**
(min 1330, max 1720) for the 200–500 mA band. But the worked design example in §10.2.2.2.2
says "KILIM = 1550 AΩ from the electrical characteristics table" — that number does not appear
in the EC table of revision N. Use **1610 AΩ** for limits ≥ 500 mA and treat the tolerance as
the real design margin.

| Target I_IN(MAX) | R_ILIM (K=1610) | E96 1 % | Actual (typ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 500 mA | 3220 Ω | 3.24 kΩ | 497 mA (CALCULATED) |
| 800 mA | 2012 Ω | 2.00 kΩ | 805 mA (CALCULATED) |
| 1.0 A | 1610 Ω | **1.60 kΩ** | 1006 mA (CALCULATED) |
| 1.46 A (max) | 1100 Ω | 1.10 kΩ | 1464 mA (CALCULATED) |

**Recommendation: R_ILIM = 1.60 kΩ (≈1 A).** Reasoning: a non-PD USB-C source advertises its
capability on CC (default / 1.5 A / 3.0 A). Setting ILIM to 1 A and having the MCU select the
ILIM state only when it has reason to believe the source can supply it gives useful headroom
for 500 mA charge + 600 mA system peak without exceeding what a 1.5 A Type-C source offers.
UNVERIFIED as a recommendation — it is an engineering choice, not a DS number. The DS 1.1 kΩ
floor is the hard constraint.

**Leaving ILIM unconnected disables all charging** (DS Table 7-1). Always fit the resistor.

### TMR — safety timers (pin 14)

DS Eq 6/7: `tPRECHG = KTMR × RTMR`, `tMAXCHG = 10 × KTMR × RTMR`. `KTMR = 48 s/kΩ` typ
(min 36, max 60). Valid range 18 k – 72 kΩ. TMR floating → defaults 1800 s / 18000 s (5 h).
TMR to VSS → all safety timers disabled.

**Design problem worth flagging.** Max programmable fast-charge timer is
`10 × 48 × 72 kΩ = 34560 s = 9.6 h` (CALCULATED). A 3400 mAh cell at 300 mA needs
roughly 11 h CC+CV — **the safety timer will expire before the charge completes, and the
BQ24074 will flag a timer fault (CHG flashing at 2 Hz)**. Note also that K_TMR min is 36 s/kΩ,
so the worst-case timer at 72 kΩ is only 7.2 h.

Options:
- **Charge at 500 mA (R_ISET = 1.78 kΩ) with R_TMR = 71.5 kΩ** (E96, → 9.53 h typ). CC+CV for
  3400 mAh at 500 mA is roughly 7 h, which fits with margin even at K_TMR min (7.15 h) —
  marginally. This is the recommended combination.
- If you insist on 300 mA, you must tie TMR to VSS and disable the safety timers, which removes
  a safety layer on an *unprotected* cell. Not recommended.
- Note DPPM also suspends charging current (see §2), which stretches real charge time while the
  timer keeps counting. The DS states TS-fault suspension pauses the timers; it does **not** say
  DPPM pauses them.

**Independent of the charger: an unprotected 18650 needs a separate protection IC / PCM**
(over-discharge, over-current, short-circuit). The BQ24074 protects the charge direction and
has an OUT short-circuit trip, but it is not a cell protection device. UNVERIFIED as a DS
statement — this is standard practice, not a DS claim.

### ITERM — termination current (pin 15, BQ24074 only)

- **Leave unconnected** → default internal threshold = **10 % of ICHG** (0.033 × ICHG in USB100
  mode). At 500 mA charge that is 50 mA ≈ C/68 — entirely appropriate for a 3400 mAh cell.
  **This is the recommendation: no part needed.**
- To program explicitly: DS Eq 5 `ITERM = 0.030 × RITERM / RISET` (USB500/ILIM modes) and
  DS Eq 4 `ITERM = 0.010 × RITERM / RISET` (USB100 mode). `KITERM` = 0.0300 typ / 0.0100 typ.
  R_ITERM range 0 – 15 kΩ, and termination is programmable up to 50 % of fast-charge current.
- Beware: the effective threshold is **3× lower in USB100 mode** than in USB500/ILIM mode.

### TS — NTC network (pin 1)

DS §9.3.6: `INTC = 75 µA` typ bias out of TS. Trip thresholds `VHOT = 0.30 V` (falling),
`VCOLD = 2.10 V` (rising), hysteresis 30 mV / 300 mV, deglitch 50 ms. Out-of-window suspends
charging and **pauses the safety timers**; CHG stays low (still indicates charging).

Bare-thermistor trip resistances (CALCULATED from the DS values):
`R_hot = 0.30 V / 75 µA = 4.00 kΩ`, `R_cold = 2.10 V / 75 µA = 28.0 kΩ`.
DS Table 9-3 row 1 confirms: 28000 Ω (−0.6 °C) / 4000 Ω (51 °C) with Rs = 0, Rp = ∞.

Because the trip *resistances* are fixed, the trip *temperatures* depend entirely on beta:

| NTC | Network | Cold trip | Hot trip |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 k, **B = 3435** (e.g. Semitec 103AT) | **bare NTC, TS→NTC→VSS, no extra parts** | **+0.5 °C** | **+50.8 °C** |
| 10 k, **B = 3950** | bare NTC | +3.5 °C | +47.2 °C |
| 10 k, **B = 3950**, widened to 0–50 °C | **Rs = 523 Ω in series (TS→Rs→NTC→VSS), Rp = 154 kΩ from TS to VSS** | 0 °C | 50 °C |

The temperatures and the Rs/Rp values are CALCULATED (beta equation for the resistances;
DS Eq 8 and Eq 9 for Rs/Rp, with VH=0.3, VC=2.1, ITS=75 µA). Exact solution Rs = 517.3 Ω,
Rp = 155.7 kΩ; nearest E96 1 % values are 523 Ω and 154 kΩ. Sanity check against DS Table 9-3:
for R_cold 33890 / R_hot 3536 the DS gives Rs=576, Rp=150000 — my B=3950 case
(R_cold 33621 / R_hot 3588) giving 517/156 k is consistent.

For B=3435 the equation yields a **negative** Rs (−89 Ω), which simply confirms the bare
thermistor already covers 0–50 °C. **DS §9.3.6: the temperature window cannot be tightened by
external resistors, only extended.**

**If you do not use TS: connect a fixed 10 kΩ from TS to VSS** (DS Table 7-1 and §10.2.2.3).
Do not leave TS floating on the '74 — the `VDIS(TS)` disable path exists only on the '72/'73.

### CE, EN1, EN2 — internal pull-downs

All three have an internal **~285 kΩ pull-down to VSS**, and the DS explicitly says
**"Do not leave CE / EN1 / EN2 unconnected to ensure proper operation."** Fit external
100 kΩ pull-downs anyway so the level is deterministic and immune to leakage and flux residue.

CE semantics: **CE HIGH disables battery charging**; OUT stays active and battery supplement
mode still works. CE LOW enables charging. Revision L of the DS specifically corrected the older
"standby mode" wording to this.

### CHG and PGOOD — open-drain status to the MCU

Both are open-drain, active low. DS specifies a **1 kΩ to 100 kΩ** pull-up to the desired logic rail.

**Critical: pull them up to the MCU's 3.3 V rail, not to OUT.** OUT sits at 4.4 V when input is
present (see §2), which exceeds the nRF52840 GPIO absolute maximum of VDD+0.3 V. UNVERIFIED
against the nRF52840 datasheet (not fetched), but the 4.4 V OUT figure is VERIFIED and the risk
is real — check the nRF52840 abs-max before routing.

Use 100 kΩ to minimise standing current (33 µA at 3.3 V with 100 k vs 3.3 mA with 1 k), which
matters for this project's power budget. The EVM uses 1.5 kΩ (R5, R6) because it drives LEDs.

| PGOOD (DS Table 9-1) | State |
|---|---|
| VIN < UVLO | Hi-Z |
| UVLO < VIN < VBAT + VIN(DT) | Hi-Z |
| VBAT + VIN(DT) < VIN < VOVP | **Low** (valid source) |
| VIN > VOVP | Hi-Z |

| CHG (DS Table 9-2) | State |
|---|---|
| Charging | Low (first charge cycle only) |
| Charging suspended by thermal loop | Low |
| Safety timers expired | **Flashing ~2 Hz** |
| Charging done / recharging after termination / IC disabled / no valid input / battery absent | Hi-Z |

Two MCU-visible gotchas:
1. **CHG is low only for the *first* charge cycle** after power-up, CE going low, or exiting OVP.
   Subsequent refresh charges leave CHG open. Do not treat "CHG high" as "battery full".
2. The 2 Hz flash is the *only* signal distinguishing a timer fault from normal idle. The MCU
   must sample CHG over >1 s to detect it. Clear the fault by toggling CE, cycling input power,
   entering/exiting USB suspend, or an OVP event.

Relevant thresholds: `UVLO = 3.3 V` typ (hysteresis 200–300 mV), `VIN(DT) = 80 mV` typ,
`tDGL(PGOOD) = 1.2 ms`.

### Capacitors

| Node | DS requirement | Recommended here |
|---|---|---|
| IN (pin 13) | 1 µF to 10 µF bypass to VSS; **"input capacitance must be < 10 µF"** to stay USB-IF inrush compliant, because this current is not limited | **1 µF X7R, 25 V or 50 V** |
| OUT (pins 10/11) | 4.7 µF to 47 µF ceramic to VSS | **22 µF X5R 10 V**, for the 600 mA class-D transients |
| BAT (pins 2/3) | 4.7 µF to 47 µF ceramic to VSS | **10 µF X5R 10 V** |

IN voltage rating: the DS rates IN to 28 V absolute max and OVP trips at 10.5 V, so a 25 V or
50 V X7R part is the right call. Remember ceramic DC bias derating — a 10 µF 6.3 V 0805 can be
under 5 µF at 4.4 V, so the OUT cap should be over-specified.

## 2. How the power path works

### OUT with an input source present

The '74 regulates OUT to a **fixed** `VO(REG) = 4.4 V` typ (min 4.3, max 4.5) whenever
`VIN > VOUT + VDO(IN-OUT)`. This is a key difference from the '72, which tracks
`VBAT + 225 mV` instead. Input-to-output dropout `VDO(IN-OUT)` = 300 mV typ / 475 mV max at
1 A — so at 5 V VBUS there is headroom, but not much: the EVM guide recommends running VIN
between **4.75 V and 5.5 V, preferring the low end**, since this is a linear regulator and
everything above VOUT becomes heat.

Priority goes to the system load, not the battery. The input current is shared, with the system
served first.

### OUT with no input present

OUT is connected to BAT through an internal FET. `VDO(BAT-OUT) = VBAT − VOUT` is
**50 mV typ, 100 mV max at IOUT = 1 A** (VIN = 0, VBAT > 3 V). At your 600 mA peak that is
roughly 30–60 mV (CALCULATED, implied R_DSON ≈ 50 mΩ typ — the DS does not quote R_DSON
directly). So **OUT follows BAT with a negligible drop**; the system rail swings over the full
3.0–4.2 V cell range when unplugged.

Consequence for the design: the system rail is 4.4 V plugged in and 3.0–4.2 V unplugged. Anything
downstream must tolerate both — a buck-boost or an LDO with low enough dropout at 3.0 V.

Sleep behaviour: when VIN is between UVLO and VIN(DT), the device sleeps and after >20 ms the
IN↔OUT FET opens, so grounding the input does not drain the battery.
`IBAT(PDWN)` sleep current into BAT = **4.3 µA typ / 6.5 µA max** at 85 °C — good for this
project's multi-day budget. Standby current into IN with EN1=EN2=HI is 41 µA typ at 6 V.

**The '74 has no SYSOFF**, so you cannot hardware-disconnect the battery from OUT. The load is
always powered from BAT when a source is absent. If a true off state matters, either add a load
switch downstream of OUT or move to the BQ24075.

### DPPM

`VDPPM = VO(REG) − 100 mV` typ (range −180 mV to −30 mV) → **≈ 4.3 V** on the '74 (CALCULATED
from 4.4 V − 100 mV).

Sequence as load rises (DS §9.3.4.1.2 / §9.3.4.1.3):
1. Charge current + system load exceed the programmed input limit → OUT sags.
2. OUT reaches V_DPPM (~4.3 V) → **DPPM mode**: charging current is throttled down as OUT
   current rises, keeping the system rail up. **Battery termination is disabled while in DPPM.**
3. Charge current hits zero and load still exceeds the input limit → OUT falls further. When
   `VOUT ≤ VBAT − 40 mV` (`VBSUP1`) the **battery supplements** the load. Exits at
   `VOUT ≥ VBAT − 20 mV` (`VBSUP2`). During supplement the BAT FET is fully on and the
   supplement current is **not regulated**.

This is exactly what carries the 600 mA class-D peaks: the peak comes out of the cell, not the
USB port, and the charger backs off instead of the rail collapsing.

Short-circuit protection in supplement mode: if `VBAT − VOUT > VO(SC2)` (250 mV typ) for
`tDGL(SC2)` = 250 µs, OUT turns off, then retries after `tREC(SC2)` = 60 ms.

### VIN-DPM

Separate loop, active **only in USB100 and USB500 modes (EN2 = 0)**. If VIN sags to
`VIN-DPM = 4.5 V` typ (range 4.35–4.63 V), the input current limit is reduced to stop the
device crashing a weak USB source. **Note this does not apply in ILIM (EN2=1, EN1=0) mode** —
the DS test condition for VIN-DPM is explicitly "EN2 = LO, EN1 = X". If you run a long or thin
USB-C cable in ILIM mode there is no VIN-DPM backstop.

### Input voltage limits, OVP, and TVS

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| IN absolute maximum | **28 V** | DS Abs Max table (−0.3 to 28 V) |
| IN recommended operating range ('74) | **4.35 V to 10.2 V** | DS Recommended Operating Conditions |
| IN voltage range (spec'd, all) | 4.35 V to 26 V | DS Recommended Operating Conditions |
| **VOVP ('74)** | **10.5 V typ** (10.2 min, 10.8 max) | '72/'73/'75/'79 are only 6.6 V |
| OVP hysteresis ('74) | 175 mV | |
| OVP deglitch `tDGL(OVP)` | 50 µs | |
| OVP recovery `tREC` | 1.2 ms | |
| ESD, HBM | ±2000 V | DS ESD Ratings |
| ESD, CDM | ±500 V | DS ESD Ratings |

The 10.5 V OVP is the main reason to pick the '74 over the '75/'72 for a USB-C port — it
tolerates a misbehaving source or a QC/PD charger briefly asserting 9 V without damage
(it shuts off the LDO and connects OUT to BAT; PGOOD goes Hi-Z).

**In OVP the system output (OUT) is connected to the battery and PGOOD goes high-impedance.**
Once OVP clears, a full power-on sequence restarts, safety timers reset, and CHG indicates a
new charge cycle.

**TVS/ESD: the datasheet does not recommend or show any input TVS.** It relies on the 28 V
absolute max rating and the internal OVP. The EVM schematic does fit **D3 = BZX84C6V2T, a 6.2 V
350 mW Zener** across the input (VERIFIED, EVM BOM Table 4) — but the EVM is a 6.6 V-OVP-centric
bench board, and a 6.2 V Zener would clamp below the '74's own 10.5 V OVP and defeat the wide
input range. **Do not copy the EVM Zener onto a BQ24074 design.**

Recommendation (UNVERIFIED — engineering judgement, not a DS statement): fit a proper
low-capacitance USB-C TVS array on VBUS, CC1/CC2 and D+/D− at the connector for ESD, chosen
with a standoff voltage above 10.5 V so it does not interfere with OVP. The IC's own ±2 kV HBM
rating is a handling spec, not a system-level IEC 61000-4-2 rating for an exposed connector.

## 3. EN1/EN2 defaults and the MCU-selectable input limit

DS Table 7-2:

| EN2 | EN1 | Maximum input current into IN |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | **0** | **100 mA — USB100 mode** (spec 90/95/100 mA min/typ/max) |
| 0 | 1 | **500 mA — USB500 mode** (spec 450/475/500 mA) |
| 1 | 0 | **Set by R_ILIM** (K_ILIM / R_ILIM, 200 mA–1.5 A) |
| 1 | 1 | **Standby — USB suspend mode.** Charging off, IN quiescent 41 µA typ |

### Reset-default recommendation

**EN1 and EN2 are internally pulled down with ~285 kΩ, so the unconfigured state is
(EN2=0, EN1=0) = 100 mA USB100 — already the safe USB default.** Nothing extra is strictly
required to land there.

**Do it anyway with external 100 kΩ pull-downs to VSS on both EN1 and EN2.** Two reasons:
1. The DS explicitly says "Do not leave EN1 or EN2 unconnected to ensure proper operation."
2. 285 kΩ is weak. If the MCU's GPIO reset state is anything other than high-Z-with-no-pull —
   and many parts default to a weak pull-**up** — you land on (1,1) = **standby**, and the
   device will not charge at all, silently. A 100 kΩ external pull-down dominates a typical
   MCU internal pull-up (13 kΩ on nRF52 is stronger, so also verify the nRF52840 GPIO reset
   state is disconnected, which it is by default — UNVERIFIED, not checked against the nRF
   datasheet).

So:

| Signal | Part | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| EN1 → VSS | 100 kΩ | reset default low |
| EN2 → VSS | 100 kΩ | reset default low |
| CE → VSS | 100 kΩ | reset default low = charging enabled |

MCU control sequence: boot at 100 mA (safe on any port), enumerate / inspect the CC pull-ups,
then drive EN1 high for 500 mA, or EN2 high (EN1 low) to use the ILIM resistor. Drive both high
to suspend charging entirely — useful if the firmware wants the full input budget for the
speaker, or to satisfy USB suspend.

**Gotcha:** in USB100 mode the internal termination threshold drops to `0.033 × ICHG` and the
external K_ITERM drops to 0.0100 — a 3× change. If firmware switches modes mid-charge the
termination point moves.

**If BQ24074 is unavailable, BQ24075 is the better fallback than BQ24072**, but read the
consequences:

1. **You lose the 10.5 V OVP.** The '75 trips at 6.6 V. On a USB-C port with no PD this is
   usually fine (VBUS is 5 V), but you lose tolerance to a non-compliant charger. This is the
   single biggest reason the '74 was chosen and the main thing to think about before swapping.
2. **VOUT(REG) becomes 5.5 V, not 4.4 V.** Your system rail rises by 1.1 V when plugged in.
   That is *worse* for a linear regulator downstream (more heat) and definitely exceeds an
   nRF52840 GPIO. Re-check every downstream input rating and the LDO dropout budget.
3. **You gain SYSOFF** — a hardware battery-disconnect on pin 15, which for a battery-powered
   handheld is genuinely useful (true off, and shipping mode). SYSOFF is internally pulled
   **up** to VBAT through ~5 MΩ, so it needs an external pull-**down** to VSS for normal
   operation, and "do not leave unconnected."
4. **You lose programmable ITERM.** Termination reverts to the internal 10 % threshold, which
   is what I recommended anyway — so this costs nothing here.
5. Pins 1–14 and 16 are otherwise identical, and the package is the same QFN-16-EP(3×3).
   A footprint that routes pin 15 to a jumper/DNP network could accept either part.

BQ24072's tracking output (`VBAT + 225 mV`) is actually the most efficient option for a linear
system rail, but it too has 6.6 V OVP and swaps pin 15 to TD.

## 5. Layout notes

From DS §12.1 Layout Guidelines and §12.3 Thermal Considerations:

- Place the **IN decoupling cap and the OUT filter caps as close as possible to the IC**, with
  short runs to IN, OUT and GND (thermal pad).
- **Keep all low-current GND connections separate from the high-current charge/discharge paths
  from the battery.** Use a **single-point ground** joining the small-signal ground path and the
  power ground path.
- Size the **high-current traces into IN and out of OUT** for the maximum charge current to
  avoid IR drops — at 4.4 V OUT with a 300 mV dropout budget, trace drop directly eats charging
  headroom.
- **Thermal pad:** internally connected to VSS and is "the main ground connection for the
  device", but §12.3 also says **"Do not use the thermal pad as the primary ground input for the
  device. VSS pin must be connected to ground at all times."** Connect both: pad to the ground
  plane, and pin 8 routed to ground independently.
- TI points to the **QFN/SON PCB Attachment Application Note** (SLUA271) for the full pad
  and stencil design. Not fetched.

**Via count — from the EVM, not the datasheet (VERIFIED against EVM §3, Table 2 footnote):**
the HPA073 reference layout uses **8+ vias in the thermal pad, 0.031-inch PCB, 2 oz copper**,
achieving **θJA ≈ 27 °C/W**. Compare the JEDEC-standard figure in the DS Thermal Information
table: **RθJA = 44.5 °C/W**, RθJC(bot) = 3.8 °C/W, ψJB = 17.1 °C/W.

Thermal sanity check for this design (CALCULATED): worst case is a nearly-empty cell at
VBAT ≈ 3.4 V with VIN = 5 V, charging at 500 mA while the system draws, say, 200 mA.
Charger loss ≈ (4.4 − 3.4) × 0.5 = 0.5 W; pass-FET loss ≈ (5.0 − 4.4) × 0.7 = 0.42 W;
total ≈ 0.92 W. At the EVM's 27 °C/W that is a 25 °C rise; at the JEDEC 44.5 °C/W it is 41 °C.
Inside a sealed 30×30×150 mm 3D-printed stick with no airflow, the real figure will be worse
than either. `TJ(REG) = 125 °C` thermal regulation will throttle charge current — which is
safe but slows charging further, compounding the safety-timer issue in §1. The DS notes
"typically after fast charge begins the pack voltage increases to ≈3.4 V within the first
2 minutes", so 3.4 V is the right minimum voltage for max-dissipation calculations.

This argues for **500 mA rather than more**, and for putting copper area under the thermal pad.
