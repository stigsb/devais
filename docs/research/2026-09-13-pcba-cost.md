**Crossover rule (VERIFIED via https://blog.poly.nomial.co.uk/2025-07-05-tips-for-getting-pcbs-made-with-jlcpcb.html, arithmetic self-consistent with the fee table):** at ≥ 17 distinct *extended* parts, Economic ($3.07/line) costs more than Standard ($1.53/line on every line, but a $25.56 setup). Our main board has ~12 extended lines out of ~28, so Economic is cheaper *on fees alone* — the decision is driven by capability, not price.

### Minimum assembly quantity
**2 pcs.** VERIFIED (C): "Economic PCBA: 2–50 pcs, 1–3 days build time"; Standard PCBA: 2–80 000 pcs, ≥ 4 days. Corroborated by the poly.nomial blog: "a minimum order of 2 PCBs, then it jumps to 5 PCBs" in increments of 5.
Economic is additionally capped at 30 pcs per single board design (VERIFIED, B) — irrelevant at qty 2.

### Economic vs Standard for the castellated radio module — the rule I could and could not verify

- **VERIFIED (C):** Economic PCBA does **not support castellated holes or gold fingers**; Standard PCBA does. This row of the capability matrix describes the *board being fabricated*, and our main PCB has no castellated edge, so it does not by itself force Standard.
- **VERIFIED (C):** X-ray is applied automatically for BGA/QFN/LGA on **both** tiers. We have a QFN-16 and a TQFN-16, so X-ray is charged either way. Economic min IC pitch 0.40 mm, min package 0402 — our QFN-16 (0.5 mm pitch) and 0603s clear this.
- **NOT VERIFIED:** I found no JLCPCB page stating that *placing* a castellated module forces Standard assembly. What is documented is a per-part flag: parts marked "Standard Only" in the JLCPCB library force the whole order to Standard (VERIFIED, poly.nomial; examples given are high-MSL LEDs, some sensors, 0201-and-smaller).
- **Decision for this costing:** the MDBT50Q-1MV2 is an MSL-3 module that will arrive via pre-order or global sourcing (loose, not on tape). Loose/odd-form parts are hand- or manually-placed. **O1 is priced on Standard** as the prudent case, with Economic shown as a lower bound. **Confirm the part's tier flag in the JLCPCB parts library before ordering.**

### Sourcing the out-of-stock radio module

Two published routes, both VERIFIED:

1. **Parts Pre-order** — https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/what-is-jlcpcb-parts-pre-order-service and https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/pcba-parts-sourcing-instruction
   - Lead time **9–15 days** to arrive in JLCPCB's warehouse; parts usable in SMT orders only, never shipped separately.
   - "Non-stock extended parts recommended to the pre-order, **minimum quantity required**." Exact price confirmed within 48 h of initial payment.
   - **Pre-ordered parts cannot be mixed with global-sourcing or consigned parts of the same part number in one PCBA order** (VERIFIED).
2. **Global Sourcing Parts** — https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/global-sourcing-parts-order-terms-conditions and https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/how-to-use-jlcpcb-global-sourcing-parts-service
   - JLCPCB buys from global distributors, priority to local authorised distributors.
   - Lead time **9–20 business days** purchasing + **1–3 business days** review. Unsupported parts cancelled and refunded within 1–7 days.
   - Handling fee: **$0.017/pin for ≤ 5 pins; max $0.078 per component for > 5 pins**; loose parts needing manual assembly $0.078/pc. No shipping charge for the parts themselves.
   - Baking fee $8 for 48 h (relevant: MSL-3 module).
   - Explicit warning: "there will be a slight difference in the price of components compared to oversea platforms."
   - Orders cannot be cancelled or returned after payment.

**R1 — the dominant risk in O1.** LCSC lists C5118826 at zero stock, pre-order ≈ $19 each against a DigiKey price of $6.15. A non-stock extended part carries a **minimum pre-order quantity** (JLCPCB does not publish the number; ESTIMATE 5–10 pcs for a module of this type). At MOQ 10 × $19 that is **$190 of module for two boards** — more than the rest of O1 combined — plus a 2–4 week wait. This single line is why O1's range is wide and why O2/O3 exist.

On that reading Aisler is not competitive for a 38-line BOM at qty 2 and only becomes sensible for small BOMs of parts Aisler already stocks. **Flagged as ESTIMATE built on a stale source — do not act on it without pricing the actual design in Aisler's calculator.**

## 10.1 Board ordering strategy — separate orders beat a multi-design panel

Three constraints decide this, two of them hard:

1. **JLCPCB's minimum single-piece board size is 10 × 10 mm** (ESTIMATE — stated in JLCPCB capabilities but not re-fetched this pass). The mic board (15 × 8 mm) and LED board (12 × 8 mm) are **8 mm on one edge and cannot be ordered as single pieces at all.** They must be panelized regardless of cost.
2. **The USB-C board is 1.0 mm thick; the mic and LED boards are 1.6 mm** (VERIFIED, BOM.md §2–4). Different thicknesses cannot share a panel. That alone forces at least two 2-layer orders.
3. **Hand-stencilling a loose 15 × 8 mm board is impractical.** A panel with rails gives the stencil something to register against. You want panels here even where JLCPCB would allow single pieces.

**The multi-design fee is avoidable entirely.** JLCPCB's "different design" surcharge applies to *distinct* designs sharing a panel. Ten copies of **one** design, panelized in your own gerber, is billed as a single design. So panelize each daughterboard 10-up yourself and submit three ordinary single-design orders. I could not verify JLCPCB's multi-design fee amount — `jlcpcb.com/help/article/panel-by-customer` returned only navigation chrome, and the quote page is a live JS calculator (NOT VERIFIED, ESTIMATE range $1.50–$8.00 per extra design). It does not matter: at $2.00 per 2-layer order the separate-order route costs $6.00 total and cannot be beaten by any panel carrying a surcharge.

**Order structure — four orders, combined into one shipment** (JLCPCB's combine-orders feature charges one freight fee):

| # | Content | Layers | Thickness | Finish | Qty ordered | Yield |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Main PCB 26 × 126 mm | 4 | 1.6 mm | ENIG | 10 pcs | 10 |
| 2 | USB-C board, 10-up panel ≈ 80 × 28 mm | 2 | **1.0 mm** | ENIG | 5 panels | 50 |
| 3 | Mic board, 10-up panel ≈ 80 × 24 mm | 2 | 1.6 mm | HASL | 5 panels | 50 |
| 4 | LED board, 10-up panel ≈ 70 × 24 mm | 2 | 1.6 mm | HASL | 5 panels | 50 |

Orders 2–4 hit JLCPCB's 5-piece minimum, so you get 50 of each daughterboard for the price of 10. That is not waste worth engineering away — it is $2 per design, and the spares absorb hand-assembly losses on the parts most likely to be ruined (the 8 mm-wide boards).

**The MHP30 is the wrong tool here.** Its 30 × 30 mm plate cannot reflow a 26 × 126 mm board — it is a rework tool, not a reflow tool. A 100 mm PTC plate costs half as much and covers each daughterboard panel in one pass, but still needs **two overlapping passes for the main board**. Low-temp Sn42Bi58 makes that acceptable: the first half sees a second excursion, and at 138 °C that is harmless. If you would rather not, a 120–150 mm plate is about NOK 700 / $76 and does the main board in one shot — the better buy if a revision 3 is likely.

The USB microscope is not optional at this BOM. You are hand-placing a QFN-16 with a thermal pad, a TQFN-16 with a thermal pad, an LGA-5 microphone and a 61-pad castellated module, with no X-ray and no AOI. A loupe will not show you a bridged QFN row.
