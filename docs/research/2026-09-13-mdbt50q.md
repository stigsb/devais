### Raytac's LF list vs Nordic's LF list — they differ

- **Nordic** (VERIFIED, S4) marks exactly these aQFN73 pins "standard drive, low frequency I/O only": **P0.02, P0.03, P0.28, P0.29, P0.30, P0.31, P1.10, P1.11, P1.12, P1.13, P1.14, P1.15**. Same 10 kHz definition.
- **Raytac** (VERIFIED, S1 §2.5/§2.6) marks all of the above **plus P0.09, P0.10, P1.01, P1.02, P1.03, P1.04, P1.05, P1.06, P1.07**. §2.6 "GPIO Located Near the Radio" explains why: these sit next to the radio inside the module, and "wrong usage may lead to undesirable performance".

Treat Raytac's superset as binding for this module. Note that P1.08 and P1.09 (pads 25, 26) are **not** in either list, so they are full-speed on this module.

### Issues that are not pad-number errors but affect the schematic

**A1 — Bootloader pin collision (VERIFIED, S12).** The stock Adafruit bootloader board `raytac_mdbt50q_db_40` (`UF2_BOARD_ID "nRF52840-MDBT50Q-1MV2"`) defines `BUTTON_DFU = P0.11` and `BUTTON_DFU_OTA = P0.12` — exactly your LED data and LED power-enable pins — and `LED_PRIMARY/SECONDARY/TERTIARY = P1.13/P1.14/P1.15`, of which P1.13 is your Type-C status pin. Using the stock board definition unmodified will fight your hardware at boot. Fork it into a custom board definition.

**A2 — P0.18 / nRESET is a plain GPIO on a blank part.** Pad 40 only behaves as a reset pin once UICR `PSELRESET[0]` and `PSELRESET[1]` are programmed to 18. Raised UICR bits need a page erase, i.e. SWD (see S10). Budget for that in the first flash.

**A3 — Battery ADC may be unnecessary.** If the design ends up in high-voltage mode (VDDH on the Li-ion rail), the SAADC `VDDHDIV5` input measures VDDH/5 internally with no external divider and no pin — freeing pad 20 (§6 below).

**A4 — Avoid the Raytac-only LF pads for anything fast.** P0.09, P0.10, P1.01–P1.07 and P1.02 are full-speed per Nordic but LF-only per Raytac. None are in your allocation; keep it that way.

**A5 — Unallocated but mandatory pads:** 1, 2, 15, 33, 55 (GND), 28 (VDD), 30 (VDDH), 31 (DCCH), 32 (VBUS), 34 (D−), 35 (D+). Covered in §3/§5.

## 3. USB pads and the normal-voltage-mode (VDD = VDDH = 3.3 V) reference circuit

Raytac §8.3 "Reg0 DC/DC and LDO Mode Disabled — Recommend using when the highest input voltage is less than 3.6 V" is the normal-voltage-mode circuit. VERIFIED, S1 p. 40 / S2 p. 42 (schematic image read at 400 dpi).

### Connections

| Pad | Net | Component |
|---:|---|---|
| 28 VDD | 3.3 V rail ("Normal Voltage input") | **C11 = 10 µF** to GND |
| 30 VDDH | **same 3.3 V rail** — tied directly to VDD | — |
| 31 DCCH | **not connected** | — |
| 32 VBUS | 5 V net | **C25 = 10 µF** to GND |
| 33, 15, 55, 1, 2 GND | GND | — |
| 34 D− | USB connector D− | **R30 = 27 Ω series** |
| 35 D+ | USB connector D+ | **R31 = 27 Ω series** |
| 17 XL1 / 18 XL2 | 32.768 kHz crystal X2 | **C12 = C13 = 12 pF**, marked *Optional* |
| 52 P0.09/NFC1, 54 P0.10/NFC2 | NFC loop | C19, C21 — **omit** if NFC unused |

Nordic confirms the mode: normal voltage mode is entered when the supply is connected to **both** VDD and VDDH (pins shorted); REG0 is then automatically bypassed and only REG1 runs.

### Series resistors on D+/D− — Raytac and Nordic disagree

- **Raytac (VERIFIED, S1 §8.3/§8.4):** shows **27 Ω in series on both D+ and D−** in every USB reference circuit.
- **Nordic (VERIFIED, S8):** Nordic employee, verbatim — *"The external series resistors are not required for D+ and D-. They are already built into the USB PHY."* A related thread notes the older DK's 39 Ω resistors were legacy and would be removed from future DKs.

Practical resolution: lay out **0 Ω / DNP-able footprints** on D+/D− so you can populate 0 Ω (Nordic-correct) and still fall back to Raytac's 27 Ω without a respin. Do not stack 27 Ω on top of the PHY's own series termination if you want a compliant eye.

### VBUS

- **5 V goes directly to pad 32.** No regulator, no divider. VBUS operating range **4.35 / 5.0 / 5.5 V** (min/nom/max); absolute max 5.8 V (VERIFIED, S1 §5.1/§5.2, reproduced from nRF52840 PS v1.7).
- The nRF52840 has a dedicated internal regulator converting VBUS to the 3.3 V used by the USB signalling interface, so **both VBUS and VDD (or VDDH) must be supplied for USB to work** (VERIFIED, S5).
- The **1.5 kΩ D+ pull-up is on-chip and software-controlled** (`USBPULLUP`). Nordic: *"This forbids the use of an external pull-up."* (VERIFIED, S5). Do not fit one.
- D+/D− are **not 5 V tolerant** (VERIFIED, S5). Nordic specifies no external components on them; ESD diodes are optional and your own call.
- Raytac's §8.5 "USB Disabled": **leave D+ and D− as NC** when USB is unused (VERIFIED, S1).
- Raytac's USB circuits show **C25 = 10 µF on VBUS**. UNVERIFIED whether Nordic mandates a specific VBUS cap value (the PS ref-circuitry page could not be fetched).

### Decoupling that Raytac requires

Raytac's reference circuits show only these external passives on the power pads (VERIFIED, S1 §8.1–§8.4):

- **VDD (pad 28): 10 µF (C11).** Present in every circuit.
- **VDDH (pad 30): 10 µF (C24).** Only in the high-voltage circuits; in normal mode VDDH is just tied to VDD and shares C11.
- **VBUS (pad 32): 10 µF (C25).**
- **DCCH (pad 31): 10 µH inductor (L4) to VDDH**, only when REG0 DC/DC is enabled. Raytac's spec: *"Recommended L4 spec: 10 µH, 0603 Chip Inductor, IDC,min = 80 mA, ±20%"*.
- **DEC1–DEC6 / DCC pins are not brought out** — they are inside the module and already decoupled. Nothing for you to fit.

Add your own 100 nF local bypass next to the module in addition to the bulk caps; Raytac does not show one but it costs nothing. (That last sentence is my recommendation, not a Raytac requirement.)

### What is already inside the module

VERIFIED, S1 §3 and the §8 preamble, verbatim: **"32 MHz crystal and RF (VDD) DC/DC inductor (Reg1) are already inside the module."** So:

- No external 32 MHz crystal or load caps.
- No external REG1 (DCC) inductor — the REG1 DC/DC inductor is internal, so `DCDCEN` can be enabled with no extra parts.
- The REG0 inductor (DCCH, L4) is **not** internal and must be added externally if you use REG0 DC/DC (high-voltage mode only).

Also VERIFIED (S1 §8 preamble), verbatim: *"Module is pre-programmed with Raytac testing code. Default is using LDO mode and need to add external 32.768 khz to work. You can use DC-DC mode without adding external 32.768 khz, they are NOT related events. When using internal 32.768 khz RC oscillator, please remove X2 / C12 / C13. When NOT using NFC, please remove NFC1 / C19 / C21."*

### External 32.768 kHz crystal

- **Pads: 17 (P0.00/XL1) and 18 (P0.01/XL2).** VERIFIED, S1 §2.5.
- **Raytac's reference circuit shows C12 = C13 = 12 pF** to GND, marked *Optional* (the crystal is only needed if you don't use the internal RC LFCLK). VERIFIED, S1 §8.1–§8.4.
- Raytac does **not** state a load-capacitance figure in the approval sheet. It says the recommended spec for the external 32.768 kHz is in the separate **Footprint & Design Guide** download (https://www.raytac.com/document/index.php?index_m_id=12). **UNVERIFIED — I did not download that package.** If you need a C_L number before ordering the crystal, pull that ZIP.
- Nordic's LFXO page states only *"To achieve correct oscillation frequency, the load capacitance must match the specification in the crystal data sheet"* and *"The load capacitors C1 and C2 should have the same value."* The numeric C_L / ESR table sits in the PS reference-circuitry section, which I could not fetch. **UNVERIFIED.**
- Note: the Adafruit bootloader hard-codes `NRF_CLOCK->LFCLKSRC = CLOCK_LFCLKSRC_SRC_RC` for **all** boards (VERIFIED, S12, `src/boards/boards.c`), so the bootloader itself never needs the crystal — only your application does.

## 4. Antenna keep-out and layout (VERIFIED, S1 §2.3, read from the 400 dpi page render)

Raytac's text, verbatim:

> "Make sure to keep the 'No Ground Pad' as wider as you can regardless of the size of your PCB."
> "No Ground Pad should be included in the corresponding position of the antenna in **EACH LAYER**."
> "Place the module towards the edge of PCB to have better performance than placing it on the center."

Dimensions from the keep-out drawing:

- **Copper-free strip: 3.8 mm deep**, measured from the module's antenna edge outward to the board edge.
- **Minimum width 10.5 mm** (the full module width), and Raytac says to widen it toward the full board width wherever possible. Their "NOT RECOMMENDED" examples show (a) ground poured right up to the antenna edge and (b) a keep-out only as wide as the module with ground on both sides — both rejected.
- The keep-out must be free of copper on **every layer**, top and bottom included.
- Module body 15.5 mm long × 10.5 mm wide, so with the keep-out the antenna end needs **3.8 mm of clear board** plus the module footprint.
- **Ground area:** pour solid ground under and around the rest of the module. Raytac's callout, verbatim: *"Please add via holes in GROUND area as many as possible, especially around the four corners."*
- Raytac offers a free layout review: PDF to service@raytac.com, subject "Layout reviewing – Raytac Model No. – YOUR company's name".

### First bootloader flash requires SWD — CONFIRMED

VERIFIED, S12:

- Repo README: *"You must have have a J-Link available to 'unbrick' your device."*
- Makefile: `FLASHER ?= nrfjprog`; `make BOARD=<board> flash` runs `nrfjprog --program … --sectoranduicrerase -f nrf52 --reset` (J-Link over SWD). `make BOARD=<board> FLASHER=pyocd flash` runs `pyocd flash -t <mcu> …` (CMSIS-DAP over SWD).
- There is **no** `flash-jlink` or `flash-openocd` target, and OpenOCD is not referenced in the Makefile. Supported flashers are nrfjprog (J-Link) and pyOCD (CMSIS-DAP or J-Link). OpenOCD would work electrically over SWD but is not a supported make target — **UNVERIFIED** as an official path.
- `flash-uf2` / `flash-dfu` targets only work once a bootloader is already resident, so they are useless on a factory-blank part.
- Raytac ships bare modules with **factory test code, no bootloader** (VERIFIED, S1 §8 preamble). Raytac's own catalog lists no bootloader-preloaded MDBT50Q SKU; the UF2 bootloader on Adafruit's MDBT50Q-RX (product #5199) is an Adafruit-added customization — **UNVERIFIED** whether Raytac offers that to other customers.

**Conclusion:** wire SWDIO (pad 51), SWDCLK (pad 53), GND and VDD to a programming header. First flash must go over SWD via a J-Link (nrfjprog) or a CMSIS-DAP probe (pyOCD). Setting `PSELRESET` and `REGOUT0` in UICR also requires SWD, for the same page-erase reason (S10).

## 6. High-voltage mode (VDDH on the Li-ion rail) and battery sensing

### (a) High-voltage mode

**VDDH operating range: 2.5 V min / 3.7 V nom / 5.5 V max** (VERIFIED, S1 §5.2, reproduced from nRF52840 PS v1.7). Absolute max 5.8 V (§5.1). A 2.9–4.4 V Li-ion system rail sits inside that window with margin at both ends.

Additional constraint (VERIFIED, S9 — Nordic DevZone): **VDDH must stay more than 0.3 V above VDD, or REG0 cannot regulate.** With REGOUT0 = 3.0 V and a Li-ion cell that reaches 3.0 V near empty, REG0 drops out of regulation at the bottom of the discharge curve. Pick REGOUT0 ≤ 2.7 V if you need regulation down to a 2.9 V cell, or accept that VDD sags with the cell below ~3.3 V.

**REGOUT0 (UICR 0x304) selectable REG0 outputs** (VERIFIED, S11 — Nordic SDK `nrf52840_bitfields.h`; the enum is absent from the fetchable docs page S7):

| Value | Voltage |
|---:|---|
| 0 | 1.8 V |
| 1 | 2.1 V |
| 2 | 2.4 V |
| 3 | 2.7 V |
| 4 | 3.0 V |
| 5 | 3.3 V |
| 7 | DEFAULT = 1.8 V (erased UICR) |

Nordic's own wording for the register (S7): *"Output voltage from REG0 regulator stage. The maximum output voltage from this stage is given as VDDH − V_VDDH-VDD."*

**On a blank device REGOUT0 reads 0xFFFFFFFF → 1.8 V.** All GPIOs then run at 1.8 V logic. You must program REGOUT0 over SWD before your 3.3 V-logic peripherals will work. Raising the field (e.g. 3V0 = 0b100 → 3V3 = 0b101) turns a 0 into a 1 and therefore needs a **UICR page erase, only possible with a debugger** (VERIFIED, S10, Nordic employee). Plan the production flow so REGOUT0 is set once at the same time as the bootloader.

**Maximum external load from the VDD pad in high-voltage mode: 25 mA**, valid when radio output power is ≤ 4 dBm (VERIFIED, S9 — Nordic employee Robin M Saltnes; this is the external draw from VDD, not the chip's own consumption). A ~1 mA PDM microphone is 4 % of that budget — comfortably fine. The thread does not cite a PS table number, so treat 25 mA as an engineering-support figure rather than a datasheet line; **the PS table it derives from is UNVERIFIED** (PS `power.html` and `ref_circuitry.html` exceed the fetch size limit).

**Raytac does document high-voltage-mode reference circuits for MDBT50Q-1MV2** — two of them (VERIFIED, S1 §8.1 p. 36 and §8.2 p. 38, both headed *"Recommend using when the highest input voltage is equal or greater than 3.6 V. Supply power from VDDH."*):

§8.1 — REG0 DC/DC enabled:

| Pad | Connection |
|---:|---|
| 30 VDDH | Li-ion rail ("High Voltage input"), **C24 = 10 µF** to GND |
| 31 DCCH | **L4 = 10 µH** to the VDDH rail (0603, I_DC,min 80 mA, ±20 %) |
| 28 VDD | **C11 = 10 µF** to GND — VDD is an *output* here, nothing else drives it |
| 32 VBUS | 5 V net, **C25 = 10 µF** to GND |
| 34/35 D−/D+ | 27 Ω series to the USB connector, same as normal mode |

§8.2 — REG0 LDO mode: identical except **DCCH (pad 31) is left unconnected and L4 is omitted**.

So the extra part count for high-voltage mode over normal mode is: one 10 µF on VDDH, and (DC/DC only) one 10 µH inductor. VDD keeps its own 10 µF and must **not** be tied to VDDH.

**USB works in high-voltage mode — confirmed.** Both Raytac high-voltage circuits carry VBUS + D+/D− (VERIFIED, S1). Nordic's own nRF52840 Dongle **PCA10059 does exactly this**: VDDH from USB 5 V, `L4 10 µH` between DCCH and VDD per the PS high-voltage DC/DC configuration, USB enumerating normally, and newer dongles ship with REGOUT0 already set to 3.0 V (0x4) (VERIFIED, S10). The USB PHY runs off its own VBUS-fed regulator, so it does not care which REG0 mode the rest of the chip is in.

### (b) SAADC VDDHDIV5

**VERIFIED, S6 (nRF52840 PS SAADC chapter).** The SAADC input selection includes **AIN0–AIN7, VDD, and VDDHDIV5**. VDDHDIV5 presents VDDH/5 to the ADC, so battery voltage is readable with **no external divider and no pin consumed**.

Stated constraint (VERIFIED, S6, verbatim): *"when using VDDHDIV5 as input, the acquisition time must be 10 µs or longer."* Set `CH[n].CONFIG.TACQ` to ≥ 10 µs.

Accuracy: the PS SAADC page I fetched gives no dedicated VDDHDIV5 accuracy figure. **UNVERIFIED** figures circulating on DevZone: divider ±1 %, internal 0.6 V reference ±3 %. Treat those as community numbers, not datasheet numbers — the PS electrical-spec tables sit in sections I could not fetch. Practical note from the same threads (also UNVERIFIED): sample VDDH during a peak-current moment, otherwise you read the unloaded cell voltage.

Also relevant: errata PAN 160 recorded VDDHDIV5 as non-functional on engineering silicon rev. Engineering A (QIAA-AA0). Raytac ships rev. 2 IC on modules with date code 914 and later, identified by the **absence** of a black dot on the shield (VERIFIED, S1 §4). Modules with one black dot are revision 1.

## 7. Pad physical form: castellated vs underside — and pitch

Source: Raytac approval sheet §2.1 "PCB Dimensions & Pin Indication" (p. 7) and §2.2 "Recommended Layout of Solder Pad" (pp. 9–12), Ver. K; identical drawings in Ver. L. I rendered these pages at 600 dpi and machine-extracted all 61 pad rectangles, then cross-checked the result against the dimensioned land pattern on p. 12.

### Headline: the premise of "all castellated" does not hold

**VERIFIED (Raytac §2.2 p. 12, dimensioned land pattern):** the land pattern is a **staggered two-row array on three sides plus a free-standing mid-board row**, and **no land reaches the module outline**. Origin (0,0) is the bottom-left corner of the 10.5 × 15.5 mm outline; the nearest land centres sit at x = 0.45–0.6 mm and y = 0.6 mm, with a 0.6 mm land length. The outer row therefore stops roughly 0.15 mm short of the edge.

**VERIFIED (grep of the full Ver. K and Ver. L text):** the approval sheet never uses the words "castellated", "half-hole", "LGA" or "hand solder" anywhere.

**Conclusion (VERIFIED by drawing geometry; UNVERIFIED as an explicit Raytac statement):** this is a **bottom-terminated LGA-style module, not a castellated one**. A castellated module's recommended land pattern always extends the land *outside* the body outline so a side fillet can form; Raytac's does the opposite. Corroborating but circumstantial: JLCPCB flags this part **"X-ray inspection required"**, which is what you would expect for hidden bottom terminations.

**Practical consequence: plan for reflow (stencil + paste + oven or hotplate). Do not plan to tack it down with a soldering iron from the side.** If you need iron-solderable side access, use a carrier/breakout board rather than this module direct-mounted.

**Caveat:** the definitive artefact is the 2D/3D drawing inside Raytac's Footprint & Design Guide package (https://www.raytac.com/document/index.php?index_m_id=12), which I did not download. If the iron-solderability question is load-bearing for your assembly plan, open that package — or ask Raytac directly — before committing.

### Pad geometry (all VERIFIED, §2.2 pp. 11–12)

- **All 61 lands are 0.6 × 0.4 mm** — the drawing is annotated verbatim `61 pad_0.6x0.4`. There are **61 lands and nothing else**: no central thermal pad, no ground slug. The middle of the footprint is empty apart from the six-land mid-board row.
- **Pitch within a row: 0.8 mm.**
- **Pitch between consecutive pad *numbers*: 0.4 mm**, because numbering alternates between the outer and inner row. This is the number that governs your fab's capability.
- **Row-to-row spacing: 0.9 mm** (outer row at 0.6 mm from the edge datum, inner row at 1.5 mm; on the right side 9.9 mm and 9.0 mm).
- Keep-out annotations on the same drawings: **"No ground pad" 12.4 mm wide × 3.8 mm deep** at the antenna end (matching §2.3), plus a small **"Toplayer no ground pad" 1.6 × 1.2 mm** window located 2.95 mm in from the left edge, just below the antenna keep-out.

### Which pads are on the outer (edge-adjacent) row vs underside-only

Derived from the measured land coordinates on p. 12. Even on the outer row these are bottom-face lands — "outer" here means edge-adjacent, i.e. the row that *would* be castellated on a castellated module and the row that gets whatever limited visual/iron access exists.

**Outer, edge-adjacent row — 33 pads:**

| Side | Pad numbers |
|---|---|
| Left (x = 0.6) | 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 |
| Bottom (y = 0.6) | 15, 16, 17, 18, 20, 22, 24, 26, 28, 30, 31, 32, 33 |
| Right (x = 9.9) | 34, 35, 37, 39, 41, 44, 46, 48, 51, 53, 55 |

**Inner / underside-only — 28 pads:**

| Group | Pad numbers |
|---|---|
| Left inner row (x = 1.5) | 5, 7, 9, 11, 13 |
| Bottom inner row (y = 1.5) | 19, 21, 23, 25, 27, 29 |
| Right inner row (x = 9.0) | 36, 38, 40, 42, 43, 45, 47, 49, 50, 52, 54 |
| Mid-board row (y = 7.2, x = 3.25/4.05/4.85/5.65/6.45/7.25) | 56, 57, 58, 59, 60, 61 |

33 + 28 = 61. ✓

The **mid-board row (56–61 = P1.04, P1.06, P1.07, P1.05, P1.03, P1.01)** is the unambiguous case: it sits in the middle of the module underside, 7.2 mm up from the bottom edge and 3.25–7.25 mm in from the left, with no edge anywhere near it. Those six are reflow-only under any reading of the drawing.

Note the numbering is not a clean odd/even alternation, because the array has gaps: on the right side the positions at y = 8.8, 5.6 and 2.0 mm are empty, which shifts the parity. Pads 49/50 are both inner, 42/43 are both inner, and 34/35 are both outer. Read the table above rather than assuming a rule.

### Bearing on the current pin allocation

Of the proposed signals, these land on **underside-only** pads: **PTT P0.02 (pad 11)**, **PWR button P0.03 (pad 9)**, **Type-C status P1.13 (pad 6 — outer, fine)**. Checking each: pad 9 → inner, pad 11 → inner. Pads 3, 4, 5, 6 (charger EN1/EN2, Type-C status) are all outer. Pads 16, 19, 20, 22, 23, 24, 27, 29 — of these **19, 27, 29 are inner**, so **I2S BCLK (pad 19), LED data (pad 27) and LED power enable (pad 29) are underside-only**. Pads 36, 37, 40, 51, 53 → 36 is inner, 37/39-adjacent 37 is outer, 40 is inner, 51 and 53 are outer.

Summarised — underside-only signals in the current allocation: **PWR button (9), PTT (11), I2S BCLK (19), LED data (27), LED power enable (29), amp enable (36), RESET (40)**. Edge-adjacent: charger EN1/EN2 (3, 4), Type-C status (5, 6), I2S LRCLK (16), XL1/XL2 (17, 18), battery ADC (20), PDM CLK (22), PDM DATA (23), I2S SDOUT (24), mic enable (37), SWDIO (51), SWDCLK (53).

Useful consequence: **SWDIO, SWDCLK and both crystal pads are on the outer row**, so the debug and LFXO connections are the most accessible ones for rework and probing.
